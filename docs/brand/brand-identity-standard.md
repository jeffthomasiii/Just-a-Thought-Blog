# Just A Thought — Brand Identity Standard

**Status:** Canonical  
**Adopted:** 2026-08-10  
**Applies to:** Website, editorial presentation, podcast, social media, resources, presentations, graphics, and future JAT brand extensions

This document defines the current brand identity for **Just A Thought (JAT)**. It supersedes older examples that treat “Blog” as part of the formal brand name or show legacy logo usage. Implementation-specific logo rules live in `docs/brand/logo-usage-guide.md`.

## 1. Brand Name

The formal editorial brand is:

> **Just A Thought**

Use **JAT** as an abbreviation only where the audience already understands the reference or where the full name appears nearby.

The website domain remains `justathoughtblog.org`, but **Blog is not part of the current brand name**.

Approved extension:

> **Just A Thought — The Podcast**

Do not add “Blog” to current logos, headings, or brand language merely because it appears in the domain or in legacy assets.

## 2. Purpose

Just A Thought is a reflective Christian publication exploring faith, Scripture, marriage, relationships, leadership, culture, technology, camping, creation, and ordinary life through a thoughtful, biblically grounded lens.

Its purpose is to help readers slow down, reflect honestly, and consider how Scripture and Christian conviction meet real life without turning every thought into a finished lesson or argument.

## 3. Positioning

JAT should feel like a thoughtful conversation with someone who has been processing something deeply and is inviting the reader into that process.

The brand is:

- reflective and honest;
- warm and conversational;
- biblically grounded;
- compassionate with complexity;
- personal without becoming performative;
- convictional without becoming combative;
- thoughtful without becoming academic or distant.

JAT is not a hot-take platform, political commentary brand, sermon-transcript archive, generic devotional factory, debate-first space, or motivational-content brand.

## 4. Editorial Identity

Authenticity matters more than polish. JAT should preserve genuine experience, relational specificity, uncertainty, curiosity, humility, and the visible process of thought.

For substantial drafting, revision, archive remediation, or AI-assisted writing, `docs/authentic-voice-ai-collaboration-standard.md` is authoritative for voice preservation. `docs/editorial-style-guide.md` governs recurring editorial conventions.

Scripture should shape the thought rather than decorate a predetermined conclusion. Distinguish exegesis, application, analogy, theological synthesis, observation, inference, belief, and speculation according to what the text or source actually establishes.

Most author-written reflections end with:

> “…just a thought.”

## 5. Visual Personality

The visual system should feel:

- calm;
- editorial;
- mature;
- warm;
- reflective;
- natural;
- grounded;
- spacious rather than busy.

The website should read more like a curated reflective journal or library than a chronological personal blog.

## 6. Core Visual Symbols

The canonical JAT logo mark combines:

- a **lightbulb**, representing thought, illumination, insight, and reflection;
- a **dove**, reinforcing peace, faith, spiritual reflection, and the Christian identity of the publication;
- an **olive branch**, reinforcing peace, growth, maturity, rootedness, and creation.

These elements are part of the established mark and should not be redrawn, rearranged, simplified, recolored, or stylistically reinterpreted for routine use.

The current light and dark marks are separate approved artworks. Dark mode is not created by applying a CSS filter or automatic color inversion to the light mark.

## 7. Color Identity

The established visual direction uses olive greens, warm neutral backgrounds, charcoal/deep ink text, and restrained warm highlights.

Current reference palette:

| Color | HEX | Primary role |
|---|---:|---|
| Deep Olive | `#4F5D3A` | Primary accent, buttons, links |
| Muted Olive | `#6F7B52` | Secondary accents, icons |
| Soft Sage | `#A8B68A` | Dividers and quiet accents |
| Warm Cream | `#F6F1E7` | Primary light background |
| Soft Parchment | `#EFE4D3` | Cards and secondary surfaces |
| Charcoal Ink | `#252822` | Primary text and dark surfaces |
| Warm Gray | `#7A7468` | Metadata and secondary text |
| Muted Gold | `#C6A15B` | Restrained highlights |

Use color with restraint. Warm neutrals should dominate; olive should be meaningful rather than ubiquitous.

Where implemented site tokens differ from these reference values, the current site design system is authoritative for production UI until this standard is intentionally revised.

## 8. Typography

**Playfair Display** is the primary display direction and is appropriate for the JAT wordmark treatment, hero titles, article titles, featured headings, and selected pull quotes.

Body and interface typography should prioritize readability and quiet contrast with the display face. The established supporting direction includes Source Sans 3 and/or Inter for body, navigation, metadata, and UI use, with Lora available where a more literary serif treatment is appropriate.

Typography should support reading rather than call attention to itself.

## 9. Photography and Image Direction

Images should generally feel natural, reflective, warm, calm, honest, slightly cinematic, relational when appropriate, and creation-centered when appropriate.

Recurring visual territory may include trails, lakes, campfires, open roads, journals, quiet morning scenes, warm natural light, olive branches, birds/doves, couples, prayer, naturally presented Bibles, mountain paths, and campsites.

Avoid overly staged stock photography, artificial expressions, unnecessarily aggressive imagery, prosperity-style Christian visual clichés, text baked into article imagery, and busy compositions that compete with titles.

For generated editorial imagery, default to no text, no logo, and no watermark. Create the image so typography or branding can be applied separately when needed.

## 10. Website Presentation

The site should prioritize:

- clear long-form reading;
- generous space;
- calm visual rhythm;
- strong but restrained hierarchy;
- soft contrast;
- simple navigation;
- clean content cards;
- integrated Scripture treatment;
- accessible mobile reading;
- curated discovery through collections, series, topics, and related content.

The homepage, navigation, footer, Listen experience, and future brand extensions should feel like parts of one publication rather than separate mini-brands.

## 11. Podcast Extension

The podcast is a brand extension, not a separate visual identity.

Canonical name:

> **Just A Thought — The Podcast**

Its tone may be more conversational and naturally spoken than the written publication while retaining the same reflective, warm, biblically grounded posture.

The canonical podcast logo is documented in `docs/brand/logo-usage-guide.md` and `docs/brand/brand-asset-inventory.md`.

## 12. Brand Governance

When brand documents conflict, use this order:

1. Current repository implementation and explicitly adopted canonical standards
2. This Brand Identity Standard
3. Logo Usage Guide and Brand Asset Inventory
4. Editorial Style Guide and Authentic Voice & AI Collaboration Standard for editorial matters
5. Older brand-kit examples and historical documentation

Do not revive a legacy logo, filename, naming convention, taxonomy, or layout solely because it appears in an older document.
